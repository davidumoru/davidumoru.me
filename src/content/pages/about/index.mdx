If you landed here, it probably means you have questions either about who I am or about this site you're on. I'll do my best to answer both.

## TL;DR

I'm David, a design engineer from Nigeria who's been building things for the web for about as long as I've had a computer.

I got introduced to programming in middle school and I've been designing since elementary school.
These days I get paid to do both. I've been sharing my work and thoughts here since April 2024.
In my free time, I like to [watch films](https://letterboxd.com/umoru/diary/).
The quickest way to reach me is probably on X [@theumoru](https://x.com/theumoru), but you can also shoot me an email at [hey@davidumoru.me](mailto:hey@davidumoru.me).

## The longer version

I was born in the early 2000s in Nigeria, where I still live. Growing up, I had access to the internet and a computer,
so I spent a lot of my childhood googling how different things worked and tinkering with things I could. I had a big thing for electronics then.
When I found something interesting, I'd dig in and try to figure it out. Which is how I still learn today.

Art came first. I grew up drawing and painting and it was something I really loved doing.
One of my favorite shows then was <RichLink href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Attack" icon={artAttack} description="A British children's television programme revolving around art, originally hosted by Neil Buchanan on CITV from 1990 to 2007.">Art Attack</RichLink>.
I was that kid that had the front and back inside cover of his books filled with doodles and sketches. In elementary school, my brother passed down his old computer, a
Dell Latitude running Windows XP, to me, and I discovered MS Paint. I'd lose hours to making raster art and drawing my favorite cartoon characters.

In the 7th grade, we had this "Intro to Python" class which was my first introduction to programming. It was a three-session class, I think,
but it was enough to hook me, even though I barely understood its capabilities; I just liked what was happening.
For my extracurricular activities in school, I was in both JETS Club, where I had the opportunity to assemble my first computer, and the Arts Club.

Fast forward a few months, and we had yet another three-or-so-class on "Intro to Web Development." The class taught the very basics of HTML and CSS.
My interest was piqued, yet again. This time, it made a lot more sense and I somewhat knew what it was capable of.
From that point on, I started making little static sites for fun. I would also go on to start designing interfaces (in MS Paint btw) for sites I wanted to build.
The CS First Club was also introduced, which temporarily became my new extracurricular activity.

In high school, I had to take Cambridge computer science classes which deepened my knowledge of computers and also made me learn a lot more about programming.
I also picked up more advanced design tools and taught myself to use them. I also had a whole 3D modelling, video editing, motion design, and photography phase but that's a story for another time.
When it was time to pick a college major, Computer Science felt like the obvious choice, and that's what keeps me employed.
I've since had the opportunity to build things professionally across code and design with various people and brands across four continents, not that anyone's counting.

## About this site

This is my corner of the internet. I'm constantly tweaking it to improve the experience. I post nicheless [notes](/posts) on whatever catches my attention,
and every so often a longer piece on software or design. Writing is still fairly new to me, but it's the best way I've found to actually understand what I think,
and a decent record of what I was thinking about at any given moment.

For information about the technology and tools behind the site, visit [/colophon](/colophon). To see what I've been up to lately, check out my [now page](/now).
